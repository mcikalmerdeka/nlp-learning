NEBULA STATION INCIDENT REPORT

Incident designation: NEB-2147-Theta
Location: Nebula Station, orbital research platform above Kepler-442b
Date of incident: 14 March 2147
Reporting officer: Dr. Elara Voss, station commander

SUMMARY

At 03:17 station time, the external sensor array on Nebula Station registered an
anomalous energy signature approximately 12 kilometers off the port-side docking
ring. The signature did not match any known stellar phenomenon, spacecraft
emission profile, or debris reflection catalogued in the Interstellar Transit
Authority database. The signal pulsed at irregular intervals, fluctuating
between 7.3 and 11.6 terahertz, and maintained a stable position relative to the
station for forty-three minutes before disappearing.

During the event, the station's primary reactor experienced a transient voltage
oscillation that caused a brief outage in sectors C through F. Backup systems
engaged without incident, and no crew injuries were reported. However, the
outage coincided with a temporary loss of communication with the surface colony
on Kepler-442b, which lasted eleven minutes and has not yet been fully
explained.

WITNESS STATEMENTS

Engineer Marcus Reid, who was performing routine maintenance in the port-side
observation module, reported seeing a "shimmering distortion" in the space
outside the viewport. Reid described the object as roughly spherical, faintly
luminous, and estimated its diameter at approximately 200 meters. He noted
that the object appeared to bend the starfield behind it, as though light were
passing through a lens.

Dr. Yuki Tanaka, the station's astrophysicist, stated that the energy signature
resembled no natural black-body radiation she had ever observed. The spectrum
showed discrete emission lines at wavelengths inconsistent with any known
element or ionized gas. Tanaka hypothesized that the object may have been
interacting with local spacetime in a way that produced measurable gravitational
lensing.

AFTERMATH

Following the disappearance of the signal, a survey drone was dispatched to the
last known coordinates. The drone returned no physical evidence, no residual
radiation, and no detectable gravitational perturbation. The incident remains
classified as unexplained. The Interstellar Transit Authority has opened a
preliminary inquiry, and the crew has been instructed to avoid public
speculation.

Commander Voss has requested that all sensor logs and personal recordings from
the incident window be sealed and transmitted to the Deep Space Research Council
headquarters on Titan. The station's reactor control system has been placed
under enhanced monitoring until the root cause of the voltage oscillation is
determined.

Dr. Elara Voss
Nebula Station, 15 March 2147
